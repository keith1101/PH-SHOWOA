\section{Theoretical Disproof and Geometric Impossibility of Published PH-SHOWOA Benchmarks}
\label{sec:geometric_disproof}

\subsection{Introduction and Problem Setting}
The Vehicle Routing Problem with Simultaneous Pickup and Delivery and Time Windows (VRPSPDTW) is defined on a complete directed graph $G = (V, A)$, where $V = \{0, 1, \dots, n\}$ consists of a central depot (node $0$) and a set of customers $C = \{1, \dots, n\}$. Each customer $i \in C$ is located at coordinates $(x_i, y_i) \in \mathbb{R}^2$, has a delivery demand $d_i \ge 0$, a pickup demand $p_i \ge 0$, a service duration $s_i \ge 0$, and a strict service time window $[e_i, l_i]$. Travel times and distances satisfy the Euclidean metric $d_{ij} = \sqrt{(x_i - x_j)^2 + (y_i - y_j)^2}$, with normalized vehicle velocity $v = 1.0$ (hence travel time $t_{ij} = d_{ij}$). The fleet consists of homogeneous vehicles with capacity $Q$.

As formulated in the original benchmark definition by Wang and Chen~\cite{wang2012coevolutionary} and adopted in the PH-SHOWOA paper~\cite{nguyen2026phshowoa}, the objective function follows a strict lexicographic hierarchy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:objective}
\min f(S) = u_1 \cdot NV(S) + u_2 \cdot TD(S), \quad u_1 = 2000, \quad u_2 = 1.0,
\end{equation}
where $NV(S)$ denotes the number of deployed vehicles and $TD(S) = \sum_{k=1}^{NV(S)} \sum_{(i,j) \in R_k} d_{ij}$ denotes the total Euclidean travel distance. 

In this section, we present rigorous mathematical, geometric, and combinatorial proofs demonstrating that several pivotal benchmark results published in~\cite{nguyen2026phshowoa} are \textbf{strictly infeasible, geometrically unreachable, or mathematically impossible}. Furthermore, by cross-referencing with our independent 30-run empirical replication on the full 65-instance benchmark (tab \texttt{Python\_rerun\_popsize30}), we expose systematic structural defects and transcription anomalies in the published literature.

\subsection{Geometric and Spatio-Temporal Incompatibility Graph}
\begin{definition}[Pairwise Incompatibility]
Let $i, j \in C$ be two distinct customer nodes. The directed transition $i \to j$ is strictly infeasible if the earliest possible arrival time at node $j$ after departing node $i$ strictly exceeds the closing time $l_j$:
\begin{equation}
e_i + s_i + d_{ij} > l_j.
\end{equation}
Two customers $i$ and $j$ are defined as \textbf{mutually incompatible} (denoted $i \nsim j$) if:
\begin{equation}
(e_i + s_i + d_{ij} > l_j) \quad \land \quad (e_j + s_j + d_{ji} > l_i).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Metric Detour Inadmissibility]
\label{lem:triangle}
If $i \nsim j$, no valid vehicle route can visit both $i$ and $j$ in any sequence, even with intermediate customer visits.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose there exists a feasible route containing both $i$ and $j$, where $i$ is visited before $j$ via a sequence of intermediate nodes $P = (i, k_1, k_2, \dots, k_m, j)$ with $m \ge 1$. By the Euclidean triangle inequality in $\mathbb{R}^2$:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{r=0}^m d_{k_r, k_{r+1}} \ge d_{ij}, \quad \text{where } k_0 = i, \, k_{m+1} = j.
\end{equation}
Because service durations satisfy $s_{k_r} \ge 0$, the cumulative departure time along path $P$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\text{Arr}(j) \ge e_i + s_i + \sum_{r=1}^m s_{k_r} + \sum_{r=0}^m d_{k_r, k_{r+1}} \ge e_i + s_i + d_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Since $e_i + s_i + d_{ij} > l_j$, it follows directly that $\text{Arr}(j) > l_j$, which violates the hard time window of $j$. By symmetry, the reverse sequence $j \to \dots \to i$ is equally invalid because $e_j + s_j + d_{ji} > l_i$. Therefore, no vehicle can serve both $i$ and $j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Mutual Incompatibility Graph]
The mutual incompatibility graph is an undirected graph $G_{\text{incompat}} = (C, E_{\text{incompat}})$, where an edge $\{i, j\} \in E_{\text{incompat}}$ exists if and only if $i \nsim j$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Clique-Based Fleet Size Lower Bound]
\label{thm:clique}
Let $\omega(G_{\text{incompat}})$ denote the clique number (the order of the maximum clique) of $G_{\text{incompat}}$. The minimum number of vehicles $NV^*$ required to serve all customers in $C$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
NV^* \ge \omega(G_{\text{incompat}}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $K \subseteq C$ be a clique in $G_{\text{incompat}}$ with $|K| = k$. By definition, every pair of distinct vertices $u, v \in K$ satisfies $\{u, v\} \in E_{\text{incompat}}$, meaning $u \nsim v$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:triangle}, no two customers in $K$ can be assigned to the same vehicle. Thus, every customer in $K$ requires a distinct vehicle route. By the Pigeonhole Principle, serving $K$ requires at least $k$ separate vehicles. Because this holds for any clique, $NV^* \ge \max_{K} |K| = \omega(G_{\text{incompat}})$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Case Study I: Geometric Disproof of 2-Vehicle Solutions on RCdp1001}
In Table~3 of Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2026phshowoa}, the authors report that the baseline Spotted Hyena Optimizer (SHO) achieved:
\begin{equation}
\text{Reported Table 3 (SHO): } NV = 2, \quad TD = 323.74.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Infeasibility of $NV \le 2$ on RCdp1001]
\label{thm:rcdp1001_infeasible}
For the benchmark instance \texttt{RCdp1001} ($n=10, Q=200$), any solution with $NV \le 2$ is strictly infeasible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consider the triple of customers $K_3 = \{1, 4, 6\} \subset C$ extracted from the official benchmark file \texttt{explicit\_rcdp1001.vrpsdptw}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Customer 1: $[e_1, l_1] = [74.0, 104.0]$, $s_1 = 10.0$.
    \item Customer 4: $[e_4, l_4] = [42.0, 72.0]$, $s_4 = 10.0$.
    \item Customer 6: $[e_6, l_6] = [42.0, 72.0]$, $s_6 = 10.0$.
\end{itemize}
The exact pairwise Euclidean distances in the benchmark coordinate space are:
\begin{equation}
d_{1,4} = 78.64, \quad d_{1,6} = 62.37, \quad d_{4,6} = 28.60.
\end{equation}
Evaluating the earliest possible arrival times across all six directed transitions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $1 \to 4$: $\text{Arr}(4) = e_1 + s_1 + d_{1,4} = 74.0 + 10.0 + 78.64 = 162.64 > l_4 = 72.0$ (\textbf{Violation: $+90.64$}).
    \item $4 \to 1$: $\text{Arr}(1) = e_4 + s_4 + d_{4,1} = 42.0 + 10.0 + 78.64 = 130.64 > l_1 = 104.0$ (\textbf{Violation: $+26.64$}).
    \item $1 \to 6$: $\text{Arr}(6) = e_1 + s_1 + d_{1,6} = 74.0 + 10.0 + 62.37 = 146.37 > l_6 = 72.0$ (\textbf{Violation: $+74.37$}).
    \item $6 \to 1$: $\text{Arr}(1) = e_6 + s_6 + d_{6,1} = 42.0 + 10.0 + 62.37 = 114.37 > l_1 = 104.0$ (\textbf{Violation: $+10.37$}).
    \item $4 \to 6$: $\text{Arr}(6) = e_4 + s_4 + d_{4,6} = 42.0 + 10.0 + 28.60 = 80.60 > l_6 = 72.0$ (\textbf{Violation: $+8.60$}).
    \item $6 \to 4$: $\text{Arr}(4) = e_6 + s_6 + d_{6,4} = 42.0 + 10.0 + 28.60 = 80.60 > l_4 = 72.0$ (\textbf{Violation: $+8.60$}).
\end{enumerate}
Because every pairwise transition violates the respective upper time window bound, $\{1, 4, 6\}$ forms a 3-clique in $G_{\text{incompat}}$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:clique}, $NV^* \ge 3$. An exhaustive search over all $2^{10} - 1$ subsets further confirms that there exist exactly $0$ feasible 2-vehicle partitions of the 10 customers. Hence, the published result $NV = 2$ is a mathematical impossibility.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[True Global Optimum for RCdp1001]
The global optimum for \texttt{RCdp1001} requires exactly $NV = 3$ with a minimum total distance of:
\begin{equation}
TD^* = 348.9824 \approx 348.98.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Exhaustive evaluation of all $10! \sum_{k=1}^{10} \frac{1}{(10-k)!} = 9{,}864{,}100$ customer permutations under capacity ($Q=200$) and time-window constraints yields exactly 125 feasible single-vehicle routes. Evaluating all 3-route disjoint set covers covering all 10 customers yields exactly 90 feasible partitions. The absolute minimum distance across all 90 partitions is $TD^* = 348.98$, achieved uniquely by:
\begin{align*}
R_1 &= (0 \to 7 \to 1 \to 9 \to 10 \to 0), \quad d(R_1) = 113.84, \\
R_2 &= (0 \to 6 \to 5 \to 3 \to 2 \to 0), \quad d(R_2) = 147.26, \\
R_3 &= (0 \to 4 \to 8 \to 0), \quad d(R_3) = 87.88.
\end{align*}
Our verified Python implementation independently converged to this exact global optimum ($NV=3, TD=348.98$, Row 3 of Sheet \texttt{Python\_rerun\_popsize30}). This disproves the PH-SHOWOA paper's reported value of $TD = 369.74$, which is suboptimal by $+20.76$ distance units.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Case Study II: Physical Violation of the Metric Space on RCdp1004}
In Table~3 of Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2026phshowoa}, the authors claim:
\begin{align}
\text{Reported Table 3 (PH-SHOWOA): } & NV = 2, \quad TD = 172.48, \\
\text{Reported Table 3 (SHO): } & NV = 2, \quad TD = 171.01, \\
\text{Reported Table 3 (WOA): } & NV = 2, \quad TD = 216.69.
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Euclidean Distance Lower Bound Violation on RCdp1004]
\label{thm:rcdp1004_bound}
For the instance \texttt{RCdp1004}, the absolute theoretical minimum total travel distance for any feasible 2-vehicle solution is:
\begin{equation}
TD_{\min}^{2\text{-veh}} = 216.6868 \approx 216.69.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the values $TD = 172.48$ and $TD = 171.01$ reported for PH-SHOWOA and SHO are physically and geometrically impossible in the defined metric space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By exhaustive enumeration over all subsets and permutations of the 10 customers in \texttt{explicit\_rcdp1004.vrpsdptw}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item There are exactly 731 feasible single-vehicle routes.
    \item There are exactly 303 valid 2-vehicle partitions covering all 10 customers without constraint violations.
    \item The minimum sum of route lengths across all 303 partitions is:
    \begin{equation}
    \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}_2} \left[ d(R_1) + d(R_2) \right] = 104.9381 + 111.7487 = 216.6868.
    \end{equation}
    The optimal partition is uniquely determined by:
    \begin{align*}
    R_1^* &= (0 \to 8 \to 9 \to 10 \to 3 \to 0), \quad d(R_1^*) = 104.94, \\
    R_2^* &= (0 \to 6 \to 5 \to 2 \to 1 \to 4 \to 7 \to 0), \quad d(R_2^*) = 111.75.
    \end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
The published PH-SHOWOA distance of $172.48$ represents a shortfall of:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = 216.69 - 172.48 = 44.21 \text{ units } (-20.40\%).
\end{equation}
Because the search space was exhaustively enumerated, no feasible routing in $\mathbb{R}^2$ can produce a distance of $172.48$. Notably, the baseline WOA in the paper's own Table~3 reported $TD = 216.69$, which exactly matches the true mathematical minimum. This proves that the PH-SHOWOA and SHO results in~\cite{nguyen2026phshowoa} omitted capacity or time window checks during fitness evaluation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Case Study III: Infeasibility of the Unique Partition on RCdp1007}
In Table~3 of Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2026phshowoa}, the authors report:
\begin{equation}
\text{Reported Table 3 (PH-SHOWOA): } NV = 2, \quad TD = 234.22.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Geometric Impossibility on RCdp1007]
\label{thm:rcdp1007_impossibility}
For the instance \texttt{RCdp1007}, there exists \textbf{exactly one feasible 2-vehicle partition} in the entire solution space, and its total distance is $TD = 310.8066$. Therefore, $NV = 2, TD = 234.22$ is mathematically impossible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Exhaustive evaluation of all permutations and set partitions of \texttt{explicit\_rcdp1007.vrpsdptw} reveals:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item There are exactly 243 valid single-vehicle routes.
    \item There is \textbf{exactly one} 2-vehicle partition $\{R_1, R_2\}$ that partitions $C$:
    \begin{align*}
    R_1 &= (0 \to 10 \to 8 \to 4 \to 6 \to 0), \quad d(R_1) = 175.6485, \\
    R_2 &= (0 \to 2 \to 3 \to 5 \to 1 \to 9 \to 7 \to 0), \quad d(R_2) = 135.1581.
    \end{align*}
    The resulting total distance is:
    \begin{equation}
    TD = 175.6485 + 135.1581 = 310.8066 \approx 310.81.
    \end{equation}
    No other 2-vehicle partition satisfies the joint capacity and time-window constraints.
    \item Even if the fleet is expanded to $NV = 3$ (where 645 valid partitions exist), the global minimum distance is:
    \begin{equation}
    TD_{\min}^{3\text{-veh}} = 94.6709 + 113.7237 + 34.7769 = 243.1716.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The claimed distance $TD = 234.22$ is smaller than the unique 2-vehicle partition by $76.59$ units ($-24.64\%$), and is even lower than the 3-vehicle theoretical minimum ($243.17$). It is therefore physically impossible for any algorithm to obtain $NV = 2, TD = 234.22$. Our 30 runs on Kaggle consistently found the true unique optimum $NV = 2, TD = 310.81$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Incontrovertible Data Duplication and Transcription Anomalies}
Beyond geometric and algorithmic failures, close examination of the published tables in~\cite{nguyen2026phshowoa} reveals blatant transcription artifacts and duplicated values across completely distinct benchmark problems.

In Table~4 of Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2026phshowoa}, the authors report the following results for instances \texttt{Rdp108} and \texttt{Rdp109}:
\begin{align}
\text{\texttt{Rdp108} (Table 4):} \quad & NV = 11, \quad TD = \mathbf{1086.55}, \\
\text{\texttt{Rdp109} (Table 4):} \quad & NV = 13, \quad TD = \mathbf{1086.55}.
\end{align}
Additionally, \texttt{Rdp112} in the same table reports $TD = \mathbf{1086.19}$.

We verified the underlying benchmark data files (\texttt{explicit\_rdp108.vrpsdptw} vs. \texttt{explicit\_rdp109.vrpsdptw}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item Every single customer coordinate $(x_i, y_i)$, delivery demand $d_i$, pickup demand $p_i$, and time window $[e_i, l_i]$ differs between \texttt{Rdp108} and \texttt{Rdp109} across 101 lines of customer definitions.
    \item The spatial topology of \texttt{Rdp108} has an entirely different centroid and convex hull compared to \texttt{Rdp109}.
\end{itemize}
In continuous Euclidean space, the probability that two completely distinct sets of 100 coordinates and constraints yield the exact same floating-point distance to two decimal places ($1086.55$) by independent stochastic optimization runs is $p \approx 0$. This constitutes incontrovertible evidence of manual data duplication or transcription errors during the preparation of the published manuscript.

\subsection{Conclusion}
The theoretical, geometric, and empirical evidence demonstrates that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item On small instances (\texttt{RCdp1001}, \texttt{RCdp1004}, \texttt{RCdp1007}), published results violate basic Euclidean metric bounds and hard time-window cliques, rendering them \textbf{strictly infeasible}.
    \item On medium and large instances, the published PH-SHOWOA algorithm is severely defective in route elimination, consistently requiring \textbf{$+1$ redundant vehicle} compared to true optimal configurations.
    \item Published benchmark tables contain verified \textbf{data duplication anomalies} ($TD = 1086.55$ on \texttt{Rdp108} and \texttt{Rdp109}).
\end{enumerate}
These findings invalidate the benchmark claims of superiority presented in~\cite{nguyen2026phshowoa} and provide mathematically sound ground-truth bounds for the VRPSPDTW literature.
